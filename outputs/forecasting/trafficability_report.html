The target

The record has no trafficability column, so it is derived

The dataset carries rainfall, temperature, humidity, wind, pressure, cloud and two soil-moisture layers. Trafficability is built from those by way of soil strength — the quantity a cone penetrometer measures in the field and the one vehicle mobility is actually specified against.

Observed
Soil moisture at 0–7 cm and 7–28 cm
Wetness
SWI, profile-weighted 0.35 / 0.65 toward the 15–30 cm critical layer
Modelled target
RCI = 300·e−2.485·SWI·f(rain), in kPa
Decision
RCI against each vehicle's cone index → GO / SLOW-GO / NO-GO

Weighting the deep layer more heavily than the surface is what a tracked vehicle actually feels: sinkage is governed by the 15–30 cm horizon, not the skin. It also means the target depends on both soil layers plus recent rainfall, so it is not a relabelling of a single input column.

The setup

No weather forecast, no peeking

Every feature at forecast origin t uses observations at time ≤ t. The only inputs known for the future are calendar and Fourier terms — an honest operational setting where no numerical weather prediction is on hand. Models emit days 1 through 7 directly rather than rolling a one-step forecast forward, so error does not compound.

Splits are strictly chronological: 2498 training origins (2015–2021), 730 validation (2022–2023) for early stopping and hyperparameters, and 724 held-out test origins (01 Jan 2024 – 24 Dec 2025) touched exactly once.

One decision shaped every result. RCI has a lag-1 autocorrelation of 0.99, so a model that predicts the level mostly learns to copy yesterday. All learned models here predict the change, y(t+h) − y(t), standardised per horizon; the anchor is added back afterwards. Persistence then becomes exactly the zero-change forecast, and the skill score reads directly as signal added beyond yesterday's value. Before this change no model beat persistence at one day; after it, nearly all do.

The lineup

Eighteen forecasts, five kinds of reasoning

Each entry below sees the same data, the same splits and the same target. What differs is the assumption each one makes about how soil strength moves. Figures are RMSE at a 7-day lead and skill against persistence.

One thing to hold in mind while reading the feature work later: because every learned model predicts the change rather than the level, none of them is being asked how wet the ground is. They are being asked which way it is moving, and how fast.

Explicit statistical structure, fitted by maximum likelihood. Cheap, interpretable, and the standard first answer for a single series.

ARIMAX-Fourier (DHR)
The same ARIMA errors, plus three Fourier pairs carrying the annual cycle as exogenous regressors. A seasonal SARIMA at period 365 is not estimable, so this is the correct form — and the harmonics are genuinely known for the future.

The decision

RMSE is not the question a commander asks

A forecast that is 15 kPa off matters enormously near a threshold and not at all in the middle of the dry season. Scoring the same predictions as the three-way call — and separately as recall on NO-GO days, where a miss sends vehicles into ground that will not hold — reorders the table.

The lowest-RMSE model is not the safest model. Ensemble(top3) catches 54% of MBT NO-GO days; plain persistence catches 62%. Squared error rewards a forecast that stays near the middle of the distribution, and a mean-reverting forecast systematically under-shoots the collapse in soil strength that defines a NO-GO day. Ranking on RMSE alone would pick a model that is worse at the one call with a real cost attached.

Why it happens

Mean signed error explains the whole reordering. A model that shades its forecasts toward the middle of the distribution scores well on squared error, and the days it shades away from are exactly the days the ground gives way.

Reading the TFT's lower quantile fixes this — and costs nothing. The TFT is trained on quantile loss, so it already emits a P10 alongside its median. Making the GO/NO-GO call against the P10 instead of the median lifts MBT NO-GO recall to 91%, the best of anything tested, and it does so while also posting the best overall accuracy (85.2%) and macro F1. That combination is the tell: this is not the usual recall-for-precision trade. The median forecast is genuinely biased high near the transitions — mean reversion pulls it toward the dry-season level just as the ground is giving way — and reading the lower quantile corrects a real bias rather than merely moving a threshold. The band it comes from is well calibrated (below), so the P10 means what it says.

Is the band honest?

A quantile is only useful if it is calibrated. Measured on the hold-out, the P10–P90 interval covers the truth about 84% of the time against a nominal 80%, and the P10 is undershot 9–12% of the time against a nominal 10% — slightly conservative, in the safe direction, and it holds in the monsoon as well as the dry season.

At one day the forecast leans on instantaneous drivers: yesterday's cloud cover — cloud suppresses evaporation, so the ground stays wet — and current surface moisture. At seven days it switches to slow accumulators: the antecedent precipitation index and the 14- and 30-day running water balance, rainfall minus reference evapotranspiration. That is the physically correct answer to each question, learned rather than imposed.

Reading it

What this does and does not establish

  • Deep sequence models earn their place, narrowly. The gap over a tuned linear model on the same 128 features is real but not dramatic. On 2,498 training origins that is the expected outcome, and it is worth saying plainly rather than overselling the architecture.
  • Pick the model on the decision, not on RMSE. The two rankings disagree, and they disagree in the dangerous direction: the sharpest regressor is among the weaker models at flagging the days the ground gives way. Whatever gets deployed should be selected against NO-GO recall at the vehicle class in question.
  • The TFT's quantiles are the practical advantage. A planner reads risk off the P10–P90 band, and the P10 doubles as a safety-biased decision rule; a point forecast at a threshold gives no way to tell a marginal call from a confident one.
  • Soil moisture here is a model state, not an observation. ERA5-Land's soil moisture is unassimilated, so the RCI target inherits that. Calibrating RCI_DRY and the decay constant needs field cone-penetrometer readings from this site.
  • One 9 km grid cell. Real off-road mobility also turns on slope, vegetation, drainage and soil texture, none of which are in this record. Treat the output as the weather-driven component of trafficability, not the whole answer.
  • The rainfall day definition changes inside the test window (IMD 08:30–08:30 IST to ERA5 00:00–24:00 IST at 2025-01-01), which adds a small inhomogeneity to the 2025 half of the hold-out.
